# F1 Race Strategy Fundamentals

## Pit Stop Time Loss

The time lost during a pit stop consists of:
1. **Pit lane entry**: Slowing from racing speed to pit lane speed limit
2. **Pit lane transit**: Driving through the pit lane at speed limit
3. **Stationary time**: Tire change duration (typically 2-3 seconds)
4. **Pit lane exit**: Accelerating back to racing speed

Total pit lane time loss varies by circuit (20-28 seconds):
- Monaco: ~24s (slow pit lane, tight entry/exit)
- Monza: ~22s (fast pit lane)
- Silverstone: ~23s
- Bahrain: ~25s
- Suzuka: ~26s

## Strategy Decision Factors

### Track Position vs. Pace
- Track position is critical at circuits where overtaking is difficult
  (Monaco, Hungary, Singapore)
- Pace advantage is more important at circuits with long straights and
  DRS zones (Monza, Bahrain, Jeddah)

### Tire Window
- The optimal pit window is when the time lost per lap from tire
  degradation exceeds the time lost from an additional pit stop
- Early pitting (undercut) works best when out-lap pace advantage on
  fresh tires exceeds the pit lane time loss
- Late pitting (overcut) works when tire warm-up is slow and worn tires
  still have good pace

### Safety Car Probability
- Circuits with narrow run-off areas have higher SC probability
  (Monaco, Baku, Singapore, Jeddah)
- Statistical SC probability varies: ~50% at street circuits, ~25% at
  permanent circuits
- Strategy should account for SC windows to minimize wasted tire life

## Common Strategy Patterns

### Undercut Mechanics
1. Pit 1-3 laps before your rival
2. Gain time on out-lap with fresh tires
3. Emerge ahead when rival pits
4. Works best with: large tire delta, good out-lap, clean air

### Overcut Mechanics
1. Stay out while rival pits
2. Gain time in clean air while rival is on cold tires
3. Pit later and emerge ahead
4. Works best with: slow tire warm-up, clean air gain, good tire
   management

### Offset Strategy
1. Start on different tire compounds than rivals
2. Gain advantage in different phases of the race
3. Common in sprint: HARD start vs. SOFT start

## Fuel Effect on Pace

Cars lose approximately 0.035-0.055 seconds per lap as fuel burns off.
At race start, cars carry approximately 110 kg of fuel. By mid-race,
the fuel-corrected pace is significantly faster. This natural lap time
improvement must be accounted for when analyzing degradation curves.
