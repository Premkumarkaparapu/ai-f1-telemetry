# F1 Tire Compounds and Regulations

## Tire Compounds

Pirelli supplies five dry-weather compounds and two wet-weather compounds:

### Dry Compounds
- **C1 (HARD)**: Hardest compound, longest lasting, slowest. Ideal for
  circuits with high tire stress. Typically lasts 35-45 laps.
- **C2 (HARD/MEDIUM)**: Hard compound, good durability. Typically lasts
  30-40 laps.
- **C3 (MEDIUM)**: Middle compound, balanced performance and durability.
  Typically lasts 25-35 laps.
- **C4 (SOFT/MEDIUM)**: Soft compound, good grip but shorter life.
  Typically lasts 15-25 laps.
- **C5 (SOFT)**: Softest compound, maximum grip, shortest life. Ideal for
  qualifying. Typically lasts 10-18 laps in race conditions.

### Wet Compounds
- **INTERMEDIATE**: For damp or lightly wet conditions. Green markings.
- **WET**: For heavy rain conditions. Blue markings.

## Tire Allocation

Each driver receives 13 sets of dry tires per Grand Prix weekend:
- 2 sets of hard compound
- 3 sets of medium compound
- 8 sets of soft compound

## Mandatory Tire Usage

In a dry race, drivers must use at least two different dry-weather tire
compounds. This effectively mandates at least one pit stop. Sprint races
have no mandatory compound requirement.

## Tire Degradation

Tire degradation is the gradual loss of grip and increase in lap time as
the tire wears. Degradation is measured in seconds per lap and varies by:
- **Compound**: Softer compounds degrade faster (0.05-0.15 s/lap vs
  0.02-0.06 s/lap for hard)
- **Circuit**: Abrasive surfaces (e.g., Bahrain, Austin) cause faster
  degradation
- **Driving style**: Aggressive cornering and heavy braking accelerate wear
- **Temperature**: Higher track temperatures increase degradation
- **Fuel load**: Heavier car at race start increases tire stress

## Tire Strategy Archetypes

- **One-stop**: Typically MEDIUM -> HARD or SOFT -> HARD. Minimizes pit
  lane time loss but requires managing tire degradation.
- **Two-stop**: Typically SOFT -> MEDIUM -> SOFT or SOFT -> HARD -> MEDIUM.
  Faster pace per stint but more pit lane time.
- **Three-stop**: Rare, used at high-degradation circuits. SOFT -> SOFT ->
  MEDIUM -> SOFT.
- **Undercut**: Pitting before a rival to gain track position through
  faster out-lap on fresh tires.
- **Overcut**: Staying out longer than a rival, gaining time while they are
  in the pit lane on cold tires.

## Graining and Blistering

- **Graining**: Occurs when the tire surface tears, creating small balls
  of rubber. Common in cold conditions or when tires are not in their
  operating window. Usually temporary and clears after a few laps.
- **Blistering**: Occurs when the inner layers of the tire overheat,
  creating bubbles on the surface. More severe than graining and typically
  permanent.
