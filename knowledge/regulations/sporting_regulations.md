# F1 Sporting Regulations — Key Rules

## Race Start Procedure

The race start follows a specific sequence: formation lap, grid positions,
five red lights sequence, and race start when lights go out. Grid positions
are determined by qualifying results. If a driver fails to maintain position
during the formation lap, they may receive a penalty.

## DRS (Drag Reduction System)

DRS is a movable rear wing element that reduces aerodynamic drag on
straights, increasing top speed by approximately 10-15 km/h. DRS can only
be activated in designated DRS zones when a driver is within 1 second of
the car ahead at the DRS detection point. DRS is typically disabled during
the first 2 laps of a race and during Safety Car periods. DRS is also
disabled in wet conditions when the Race Director deems it unsafe.

## Safety Car

The Safety Car is deployed when track conditions are deemed unsafe for
racing at full speed. During Safety Car periods, drivers must maintain
position behind the Safety Car and reduce speed significantly. The Safety
Car period typically lasts 3-5 laps. Pit stops are permitted during Safety
Car periods, and strategic teams often use Safety Car deployments to make
"free" pit stops with reduced time loss. Lapped cars may be allowed to
unlap themselves before the restart.

## Virtual Safety Car (VSC)

The Virtual Safety Car requires drivers to reduce speed by approximately
40% across all mini-sectors. Unlike a full Safety Car, the VSC maintains
the gaps between drivers. Pit stops during VSC offer a smaller time
advantage compared to full Safety Car stops but are still strategically
beneficial.

## Pit Stop Regulations

Each driver must use at least two different dry-weather tire compounds
during a dry race. The minimum pit stop time is determined by the speed
limit in the pit lane (typically 60-80 km/h depending on circuit). Unsafe
releases from the pit box result in penalties. The pit lane time loss
varies by circuit, typically ranging from 20-28 seconds.

## Track Limits

Drivers must keep at least two wheels within the white lines defining the
track at all times. Lap times achieved with track limit violations are
deleted. Repeated track limit violations result in black-and-white flags
(warning) and eventually time penalties.

## Penalties

Common penalties include: 5-second time penalty (minor infractions),
10-second time penalty (more serious), drive-through penalty, stop-and-go
penalty (10 seconds), and disqualification. Grid penalties are applied for
engine component changes beyond the allowed allocation.

## Flag Rules

- Green flag: track is clear
- Yellow flag: danger ahead, no overtaking
- Double yellow: great danger, be prepared to stop
- Red flag: session stopped immediately
- Blue flag: faster car approaching, let them pass within 3 flags
- Black flag: disqualification
- Black and white flag: warning for unsportsmanlike behavior
- Chequered flag: end of session
